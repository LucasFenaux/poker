# Reach-Weighted On-the-Fly Bootstrapped Distillation

## Overview
This algorithm is a compute-efficient, ultra-low variance alternative to standard Neural Fictitious Self-Play (NFSP). Instead of relying on noisy Behaviour Cloning (BC) on hard actions sampled from a Reservoir Buffer, it uses distillation to learn from the exact action probabilities of historical models. To maintain mathematical correctness for Extensive-Form games, it samples full trajectories (hands) to inherently compute the exact reach-weighted historical average policy (as required by Deep NeuRD) on the fly.

## Core Components
1. **Online Data Storage (Casino Manager):** A short-term, sliding-window buffer that collects the newest full trajectories (hands) generated by the current Best Response model during self-play.
2. **Trainer Reservoir Buffer (Async Trainer):** A dedicated, long-term buffer maintained by the Async Trainer. It pulls trajectories from the Casino Manager's short-term buffer and uses Reservoir Sampling to store them. This ensures the training data uniformly represents all states ever visited across training, preventing catastrophic forgetting of older subtrees.
3. **The Average Network:** A neural network trained to approximate the reach-weighted historical average policy.
4. **The Bootstrapping Anchor:** A frozen snapshot of the Average Network, taken every $k$ checkpoints, representing all models prior to the current $k$ window.
5. **The Async Trainer:** A dedicated Ray actor that manages the Reservoir Buffer, continuously samples batches of hands from it, and updates the Average Network.

## The Training Process
The Async Trainer performs the following steps for each batch of hands sampled from its **Reservoir Buffer**:
1. **Process the Sequence:** For a sampled sequence of states $S_0 \rightarrow A_0 \rightarrow S_1 \rightarrow A_1 \dots$, the trainer evaluates the Anchor Network and the $k$ most recent historical models sequentially across the hand.
2. **Track Reach Probabilities:** For each model $i$, it maintains a running product of the probabilities of the actions *actually taken* in the hand. 
3. **Compute the Target:** At any state $S$ in the hand, the distillation target is computed as the mathematically exact reach-weighted average:
   $$Target(S) = \frac{\sum_i \eta_i(S) M_i(S)}{\sum_i \eta_i(S)}$$
   where $\eta_i(S)$ is the model's reach probability up to state $S$.
4. **Update:** The Average Network is trained via Cross-Entropy Loss against the $Target(S)$ probabilities (using the targets as soft labels).

## The Bootstrapping Anchor & Kuhn's Theorem
To avoid running thousands of historical models during the batch evaluation, the algorithm leverages a **Bootstrapping Anchor**. 
The Anchor Network represents the average of the first $T-k$ models. By evaluating the Anchor Network alongside the recent models, its calculated reach probability acts as a **telescoping product**. 

According to Kuhn's Theorem, the reach probability of a perfectly averaged Behavioral Strategy exactly equals the average of the individual models' reach probabilities. Therefore, the Anchor's sequentially calculated reach probability perfectly represents the true average historical reach, allowing for an exact $O(1)$ target calculation:
- **Anchor Weight:** $(T - k) \times \eta_{anchor}(S)$
- **Recent Models Weight:** $\sum_{j=1}^k 1.0 \times \eta_{recent\_j}(S)$

### Mitigating Recursive Drift
Because the Anchor iteratively learns from targets generated by previous versions of itself, the algorithm is susceptible to compounding approximation errors and progressive skill forgetting. To reset this theoretical drift and maintain long-term mathematical stability, the algorithm periodically employs model recalling.
During these periodic check-ins, the Average Network is regularized using the true average of historical models, or by applying hierarchical distillation and parameter-averaging techniques like Stochastic Weight Averaging (SWA) or Population Parameter Averaging (PAPA) across a subset of past anchors. This establishes a ground truth to preserve tail knowledge while retaining the $O(1)$ efficiency of the Anchor for standard updates.

## Engineering Requirement: The Log-Probability Trick
Because poker hands can extend for 20+ actions, maintaining a running product of probabilities $\eta_i(S) = \prod \pi_i(a)$ will quickly result in catastrophic floating-point underflow (all reach probabilities rounding to exactly `0.0`), causing `NaN` targets.

To survive floating-point collapse, the entire target calculation must be performed in **log-space**.
1. **Log-Reach Accumulation:** Instead of multiplying probabilities, sum the log-probabilities.
   $$\log \eta_i(S_t) = \log \eta_i(S_{t-1}) + \log M_i(A_{t-1} | S_{t-1})$$
2. **Log-Space Target Calculation:** To compute the final weighted target logits, use the `LogSumExp` trick.
   Let $w_i = \log \eta_i(S)$ (adjusted by $+ \log(T-k)$ for the Anchor).
   Let $l_{i, a}$ be the log-probability of action $a$ from model $i$.
   The log-probability of the target for action $a$ is:
   $$\log Target(a|S) = \text{LogSumExp}_i(w_i + l_{i, a}) - \text{LogSumExp}_i(w_i)$$
   You can then exponentiate these values back into standard probabilities (or pass the log-probabilities directly, depending on your framework) to feed into your Cross-Entropy loss function.
